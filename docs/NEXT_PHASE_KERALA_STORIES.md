# Next phase proposal: Kerala Behind the World

Status: the three-anchor photo-story pilot has been implemented; see
[pilot notes](KERALA_STORY_PILOT.md). Further expansion remains a design proposal.
The [Kerala content roadmap](KERALA_CONTENT_ROADMAP.md) incorporates the user's
next cultural and historical priorities. The living-world work remains the foundation.

## Direction

Make the playable world a doorway into real Kerala. A player notices something,
spends time with it, then optionally discovers the real place, people or practice
behind it. Real photographs provide recognition and context; they do not replace
the stylised world or interrupt every discovery.

WORLD → LIFE → CURIOSITY → EXPLORATION → REAL CONNECTION → MEMORY

The next phase should deepen a few existing places before expanding geography.
The cinema suggestion becomes one strand of Kerala culture alongside everyday
life, food, landscape, architecture, art and contemporary youth culture.

## Experience layers

| Layer           | Experience                                    | Example proposal                                                                               |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Notice          | Something belongs naturally in the scene      | A framed photograph in a tea shop; an illustrated boat shed wall; a cinema noticeboard         |
| Investigate     | Player chooses to inspect or speak            | A subtle nearby action appears; proximity alone never opens a reading panel                    |
| Connect         | Briefly meet the real Kerala behind the scene | One photograph, one memorable fact and an explanation of what makes this subject distinctive   |
| Explore further | Optional depth and another reason to wander   | A related livelihood, landscape or architectural detail the player can notice elsewhere        |
| Remember        | Save a personally meaningful connection       | Keep a photo story in the existing passport alongside the player's own visit and circumstances |

Use two image presentations: lightweight images on physical objects in the 3D
world, and an accessible close-reading view opened deliberately. Keep movement
and camera stable; pause the single-player simulation while reading, then resume
it at the same moment. Never require reading, quizzes or an image download to
continue a journey.

## Authenticity and editorial boundaries

Kadal is a fictional, compressed setting. Its stories should say “inspired by”
or “related real-world example” unless a location is intentionally mapped and
verified as a recreation. Separate facts, local accounts, film references and
fictional dialogue visually. A film scene is not evidence about a community.

Each story record needs a stable ID, subject and district, short introduction,
source links, review date, reviewer status, image credit, permission/licence
record, image description, Malayalam/English text and related world anchors.
Keep changeable visitor information out of the first release; do not represent
prices, hours or routes as current without an update process.

Build a small reviewed collection. Candidate evidence includes official cultural
institutions, archives and first-hand contributors. Verify the precise claim and
image provenance before publishing. Rights-cleared assets may be commissioned,
contributed with permission or sourced under suitable documented terms. Do not
assume that a tourism page, search result or social post grants image reuse.

Use authored content rather than live-generated factual explanations. Malayalam
wording and the portrayal of local practices need review by people familiar with
the subject. Avoid flattening Kerala into a single food, religion, dialect or
cinema fandom.

## Cinema and younger audiences

Aim for recognition, participation and social texture, rather than celebrity
images scattered as collectables. A fictional local film club can host a changing
noticeboard, a discussion near the tea shop or a small voluntary scene-guessing
activity. Participation should make a place memorable without becoming a task
required for progress.

Possible later experiments:

- Recognise a filming location from an approved still and compare it with the
  real landscape. Include film/year/location only after verification.
- Discover Malayalam dialogue with optional translation and context, using
  cleared excerpts. Do not turn an actor's image into an endorsement of the game.
- Recreate a mood using original characters, camera angles and original dialogue.
  A photo mode could let players compose their own Kerala moments.
- Add contemporary interests beyond cinema: football at a local ground, music
  rehearsal, photography, cafe conversation and short scenic scooter rides.

For the initial pilot, use original fictional film-club material. Real actor
portraits, stills, posters, dialogue recordings or clips are a separate content
workstream with documented permissions and editorial review. Do not build the
pilot around their availability or imitate actors' voices.

## Recommended pilot

Build three story anchors in places already functioning:

1. Fishing shore: connect the observed catch/delivery cycle with a reviewed real
   coastal-work story and photograph. Distinguish game timing from real work.
2. Coir yard: show material and craft details that give Radha's work more meaning.
3. Tea shop: a small local photo wall connecting everyday life and place; include
   one original fictional film-club notice as an optional tone experiment.

Budget: three anchors, at most two images per factual story, short bilingual
introductions, optional deeper reading, source/credit access and passport saving.
No map expansion, new currency, backend, infinite feed, autoplay media, celebrity
collection, mandatory quizzes or large cinematic cutscenes in this pilot.

Implementation sequence:

1. Choose stories and obtain reviewed text and usable images before placing them.
2. Add a shared story-data format and reusable physical inspection interaction.
3. Add the close-reading view, translation, credits and passport references.
4. Place the three anchors and test noticing, reading and resuming exploration.
5. Evaluate one film-club experiment separately; expand it only if it fits the
   world's tone and its content can be maintained.

Keep image files outside saved progress; saves retain story IDs and visit context.
Use small world textures, load larger photographs only when inspected, cap cached
textures and dispose of them on exit. Missing images should leave usable text.
Reuse the existing passport and portal rather than creating a second collection UI.
Do not add automatic public sharing or analytics for the pilot.

## How to decide whether to continue

First finish the combined manual check of village life and smooth transport.
Then observe short sessions without telling players where story anchors are.
Look for voluntary inspection, correct distinction between fictional and real
places, recall of one meaningful detail, and renewed exploration after reading.
Check muted play, text size, Malayalam layout, slow loading and a physical phone.

If players repeatedly close the panel to return to the game, shorten the stories
or move more meaning into physical details. If film content overwhelms local life,
reduce its prominence. Publish no story lacking reviewed sourcing and a recorded
basis for image use. These are proposed project quality gates, not a request for
approval to implement now.

Recommended outcome: “I saw it happen here. Now I understand something real about
Kerala—and I want to look more closely at the next place.”
