# Kerala content roadmap

Design update: 8 September 2026. Incorporates the user's cultural and historical
suggestions. This is a proposed expansion beyond the implemented photo-story
pilot; the features below are not yet implemented.

## Principle

WORLD → LIFE → CURIOSITY → EXPLORATION → DISCOVERY → MEMORY

Every subject needs a reason to exist in a place before it needs a reading card.
A long tea pour, a crowded television corner, a path to a football ground or an
old photograph should invite attention. Reading remains optional. Kadal is a
fictional setting; clearly name the actual place behind each historical story.

## Everyday life: the next implementation batch

Deepen the existing tea shop first. Connect these behaviours to the existing
clock, weather, residents and transport arrivals rather than starting a separate
activity system.

| Element | World behaviour | What invites exploration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meter chaya | Leela performs the long pour while serving an actual waiting customer, with a brief utensil sound | A recognisable gesture glimpsed from the road |
| Regional food | Shop profiles choose a small, locally reviewed menu; stock changes with preparation, sales and time | Different trays and conversations make places distinct |
| Television corner | A small original or cleared football/cinema programme attracts interested residents when they have time; seating limits the gathering | Commentary, laughter and people looking toward the same corner |
| Social conversations | Short exchanges respond to food, weather, an arrival or what is on television | Listening offers context without assigning a mission |

Food candidates: parippuvada, sukhiyan, pazham pori, ela ada, unnakkaya and pathiri.
Review their serving context locally, including whether an item belongs in a
snack tray or a meal. Regional profiles express tendencies, not exclusive district
ownership. Do not randomly scatter all items into every shop.

The TV is a world object with restrained directional sound and optional captions.
Use original material initially; actual footage, dialogue and broadcasts require
documented reuse rights. It does not open a separate entertainment menu. Residents
can leave to catch transport or return to work; the programme continues without
the player. Weather may draw people under shelter, subject to available space.

Acceptance: the player can notice the shop socially from outside, watch without
interacting, and return at another time to a different believable situation.

## Neighbourhood identity: following batch

Mosques, temples and churches should be functioning neighbourhood places. Give
each an appropriate approach, architecture, paths, opening/activity rhythms and
local relationships, reviewed for the represented community. Begin with exterior
life and pedestrian connections; add interiors only with researched layouts and
customs. Avoid placing identical sets of three in every settlement.

Religious plurality should also be visible in shared everyday places: shopping,
transport, work, friendships and mutual aid. Religious identity should not dictate
a character's personality. Use source-linked, restrained sound schedules and
preserve quiet periods; provide captions and volume controls. Specific festival
dates and observances require a maintained calendar rather than random triggers.

Add Malabar sevens football where the regional setting supports it. A usable
ground, available players, organisers, daylight/lighting and weather determine
whether a game can happen. People arriving, cheers and spectators returning to
the tea shop create connections between places. A missed match may leave an
ordinary aftermath: a conversation, a notice or people packing away equipment.
Start with one small gathering; do not require a full playable football game.

## Remembering difficult events

Historical disasters belong in deliberate, quiet encounters. Offer a clear subject
label before opening, concise non-graphic text, source access and an easy exit.
Do not turn outbreaks or disasters into ambient entertainment, rescue scores,
collect-all challenges or randomly recurring gameplay events. Avoid invented
survivor testimony and implying fictional residents are real historical people.
First-person contributions require consent and careful review.

A player may privately keep a story in the existing passport. Do not attach money,
rarity, achievement streaks or completion pressure to remembrance. The meaningful
reward is understanding and a remembered connection to place.

## Production order and review

1. Tea pouring, a locally reviewed food tray and the shared TV corner.
2. Faith-space exterior life and one regional sevens gathering, integrated with
   resident schedules, travel and weather.
3. A small reviewed story release: Thumba, Chemmeen and Silent Valley, plus the
   fishermen's flood-rescue account when suitable material is ready.
4. Carefully reviewed remembrance stories for Nipah, Wayanad and the tsunami.

Keep per-batch manual checks in KADAL_PLAYTEST.md as implementation lands. Verify
muted play, readable captions, natural arrivals/departures, no crowd obstruction,
stable ride cameras, phone performance and return visits at different times.
These checks are pending until their corresponding features exist.

Useful user contributions, whenever available: a preferred regional reference for
the first neighbourhood; familiar shop menus and Malayalam expressions; personal
photos with location/date and permission; and local corrections to faith-space
details. No additional input is required to continue the design work.
